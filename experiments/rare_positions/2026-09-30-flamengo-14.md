## Necessary conditions

Flamengo currently has **60 points, 18 wins, and 10 games left**. Its phase sorts
by `pt,w,gd,gf,gp,name`. Finishing 14th requires 13 rivals ahead.

For an impossibility proof we can force Flamengo to lose every remaining game.
Converting its wins/draws to losses reduces its points/wins and improves the
opponent's points/wins. Every previously ahead rival remains at least equal in
points/wins afterward. Thus a possible 14th-place season would imply a season
with Flamengo at 60/18 and at least 13 rivals at or above 60/18.

This is a necessary relaxation, not a reachability claim. A rival tied on both
points and wins is generously counted as ahead, regardless of goals. A rival
with more than 60 points qualifies even with fewer than 18 wins.

Four rivals cannot reach even 60 points:

| Remo | 110 | 23 | 10 | 53 |
| Chapecoense | 318 | 18 | 11 | 51 |

That leaves 15 eligible rivals. We test every set of 13 required ahead:
`C(15,13) = 105`. Each set uses shared fixture outcomes, so rivals cannot win
the same game against each other.

## Concrete conflict that the points-only model misses

Corinthians (24) and Mirassol (584) each have 32 points, 8 wins and 10 games left.

- Nine wins and one draw produce **60 points / 17 wins**: below Flamengo.
- Both therefore need **ten wins**, producing 62 points / 18 wins.
- They face each other in fixture **364121**; both cannot win it.

Consequently at least one must be excluded from the ahead set. This example
explains much of the pruning, but does not by itself refute every possible set.
The exhaustive fixture search handles the other alternatives. The most costly
set excludes Vasco and Corinthians; it takes 1,630 nodes to refute.

### Why excluding Vasco and Corinthians is the hardest set

Here those two are allowed to stay behind Flamengo; the other 13 eligible
rivals are all required ahead. This removes Corinthians' ten-win requirement
and Vasco's at-least-ten-win requirement. Mirassol still must win all ten, but
does not play Botafogo or Vitória in the remaining fixture list. Its forced wins
therefore do not immediately contradict those two particularly demanding rivals.

Endpoint propagation fixes only 20 of 103 fixtures at this root: Flamengo's ten
losses and Mirassol's ten wins. Examples of the remaining alternatives:

- Vitória must win at least nine of its ten games; its one other result may be
  a draw or a loss.
- Botafogo can qualify with eight wins and two draws (61 points / 17 wins), or
  with nine wins and a loss (62 / 18).
- Bragantino and São Paulo each need at least eight wins from eleven games.
  Each loses to Mirassol, but can still satisfy its threshold individually.

Those requirements become inconsistent jointly through shared fixtures. A
separate root check of every subset's minimum-win demand against the maximum
available shared-fixture wins found no violation, so minimum wins alone do not
certify this case either; the draws/points alternatives also matter.

An instrumented independent DFS reproduces the 1,630 nodes. The branching rule
uses domain size and individual slack, without conflict learning. Consequently
it repeats some fixture choices under different earlier assignments:
Botafogo–Vasco (364080) is selected 57 times; Botafogo–São Paulo (364152), 43.
Its deepest explored path has 31 fixture decisions. These are limitations of
this search order and propagation, not evidence that 1,630 nodes are necessary
for every sound algorithm.

## Why production leaves it undecided

The outer rank screen checks points and applicable wins. The inner constructive
search and joint point-floor proof still model raw points. Points-only joint
propagation admits consistent domains; it does not establish a complete season
with the required wins. Its cohort search stops at such consistent domains.

The prior 100-node constructive diagnostic generated 27 canonical complete
seasons for this cell, with actual rank 8. In its first season six rivals tied
Flamengo on 60 points, and three had only 17 wins. Goal changes cannot repair
that deficit. See [earlier diagnostic](2026-09-30-rust-reachability.md).

Increasing only the cohort-proof budget cannot solve a points relaxation that
is feasible. The improvement needed here is joint wins propagation plus fixture
branching until every necessary ahead set is refuted.

## Offline method and measurements

The Rust diagnostic encodes the lexicographic pair as `29 * points + wins`.
29 exceeds every possible final win total in this request. Win/draw/loss gains
are consequently 88/29/0, and the target floor is 1,758. It reuses production
endpoint and aggregate propagation, branches on remaining fixture domains, and
disables the raw 3/1/0 discrete cut for this transformed scoring system.

The independent Python checker rebuilds points/wins from the request and uses
endpoint propagation only. Both allow all three outcomes of every unfixed game.

Seven sequential release Rust runs per configuration, one CPU thread; median
wall times below. Budgets are **total nodes across cohorts**. They are not
directly equivalent to existing production per-cell budgets.

work; production adoption must replace/reallocate existing work and be measured
on paired full requests with the existing four-worker limit. No extra latency
allowance is assumed.
