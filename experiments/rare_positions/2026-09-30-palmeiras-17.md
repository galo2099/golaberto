# Palmeiras / 17th: points-only impossibility certificate

## Result and scope

For saved group **16498**, snapshot `group-16498-44eabb47.json`, Palmeiras (16)
**cannot finish 17th or lower**. The shipped production estimator at `4333d31b` calls 17th
`undecided`; this analysis supplies the missing impossibility proof. Its worst
reachable position is **16th**, for which the enabled goal-completion code
already has a verified witness.

This is a deterministic fixture/points proof, not a probability estimate or
a confidence bound. It allows arbitrary legal score margins and does not use
a goal cap. It applies to this saved request, not automatically to a later
database snapshot. This original analysis changed no production algorithm or
cell label. The subsequent implementation and paired results are in
[the discrete cut report](2026-09-30-rust-discrete-cut.md).

The request uses `pt,w,gd,gf,gp,name`, standard 3/1/0 points, no bonus points or
team point adjustments. Names were read from the local DB without writes.

## Necessary conditions

Palmeiras already has **57 points / 16 wins**, with ten games remaining. It
cannot finish with fewer than 57 points. Every rival ahead must therefore have
at least 57 points. We relax all later tiebreakers in rivals' favor, so
infeasibility at this point floor proves impossibility under the actual sorter.

Remo (110) has at most **53** points and Chapecoense (318) at most **51**. Both
must stay below Palmeiras. Seventeenth requires 16 rivals ahead out of 19, so
only **one additional rival** may be below.

Internacional (20) starts with 28 points and has ten games left. Nine wins and
a draw yield 56: it must win **all ten** to reach 57. Grêmio (79) starts with
29 points and has ten games left. One loss leaves a maximum of 56: it cannot
lose any game if it reaches 57. They meet in game **364094**, Grêmio–Inter.
Both cannot reach 57. Consequently the sole additional below-Palmeiras team
must be **Inter or Grêmio**. The following two cases exhaust the possibilities.

## Case A: Grêmio is below; Inter reaches 57

1. Inter must win everything, including its games against Corinthians (24),
   Mirassol (584), Vitória (67) and Vasco (18).
2. Corinthians and Mirassol each start with 32 points and have ten games left.
   After losing to Inter, neither can afford another loss: eight wins and two
   losses yield only 56. Their head-to-head **364121** must therefore be a draw.
3. With one loss and that draw, each needs to win all eight other games to
   finish on 57. Corinthians must beat both Vitória (**364103**) and Vasco
   (**364117**).
4. Vitória starts with 33 points and ten games left. Losses to Inter
   (**364155**) and Corinthians force eight wins to reach 57. Thus Vitória
   must beat Vasco (**364178**).
5. Vasco starts with 31 points and eleven games left. Losses to Inter
   (**364147**), Corinthians and Vitória leave at most eight wins:
   **31 + 8×3 = 55**.

Vasco is an additional rival below Palmeiras, contradicting the allowed one.
This case is impossible using only points and the shared fixtures.

There are **32 remaining games between these teams** and **51 against teams
outside the set**: 83 distinct games involving at least one member. Each can
give the set at most three points. Its optimistic maximum final total is:

```text
380 + 3 × 83 = 629
```

Eleven teams each needing 57 require **627**, leaving only two points of slack.
An internal draw gives the set two points instead of three, consuming one
point of slack. Therefore there can be **at most two internal draws**. An
outside draw consumes two points of slack; an outside loss consumes three.

But without draws the individual teams need **87 wins**, and the 83 incident
games can supply at most **83 wins** to them. Each internal draw can reduce
the required win count by at most two (one draw point for each team), while
also removing one available win. Its net benefit is at most one. An outside
draw can save at most one required win and removes one available win, giving
no net benefit. Consequently at least **four internal draws** are needed to
close the four-win shortfall.

**At least four internal draws, but at most two are allowed.** This case is
also impossible. No assumption about wins as a tiebreaker is needed; wins here
are simply how three-point results are generated.

### General form of the discrete aggregate cut

For a coalition S, point floor F, current-point sum B, internal-game count I,
boundary-game count E, and M = I + E:

```text
Q = sum over t in S of max(0, ceil((F - current_points[t]) / 3))
slack = B + 3M - F × |S|
internal_draws >= max(0, Q - M)
internal_draws <= slack
```

If the lower bound exceeds the upper bound, the coalition cannot all reach F.
This combines point conservation with the integer nature of three-point wins.
It is generic for standard 3/1/0 scoring. Passing the cut does not establish
joint feasibility or reachability.

## Why the current Rust algorithm misses this

- The early floor solver checks point-domain propagation and a selected family
  of aggregate point cuts. A consistent relaxation ends the query: it does not
  exhaustively assign fixtures. The earlier Palmeiras queries returned such
  a relaxation after 13 nodes; the 500-node limit was not exhausted.
- Current aggregate cuts compare summed point demand/capacity for selected
  slack-ordered subsets. They do not include the draw/win rounding cut above.
  The critical eleven-team subset is not guaranteed to be one of those subsets.
- The constructive minimum-points witness search has only 100 nodes per cell.
  Earlier diagnostics reached that limit with zero complete seasons and 46
  propagation contradictions for this cell. Goal completion can only help
  after a complete outcome assignment is found; it cannot overcome this point
  contradiction.
- An independent offline exact point-floor DFS explored 1,236 nodes in the
  only case that survives basic propagation (Inter below), and refuted it.
  The other 16 choices of the additional below team were rejected by basic
  propagation. The full Python diagnostic took about 0.44 seconds locally;
  that is offline computation, not extra production budget.

The full request's existing `zero_hit_upper_95` for seed 808 is approximately
`3.01e-9` in probability units. This proof establishes the true event probability
is **zero for this snapshot**; the prior confidence limit is no longer the best
available conclusion. The shipped server will still report `undecided` until
a generic production proof improvement is implemented and measured.

## Native verification and reproduction

The offline Rust example reads the same `Model::new` as production, including
point adjustments, and recomputes the certificate directly from its campaigns
and fixtures. It does not access MySQL, run sampling, change labels, or run as
part of an HTTP request. The supplied subset is a certificate, not a claim
that discovering the right subset takes the same time.

```sh
cargo test --release --locked --manifest-path odds-rust/Cargo.toml \
  --example point_floor_certificate
cargo run --release --locked --manifest-path odds-rust/Cargo.toml \
  --example point_floor_certificate -- \
  experiments/rare_positions/reference/2026-09-30-hundredfold/inputs/group-16498-44eabb47.json \
  57 4,6,7,10,14,18,24,67,79,225,584
```

Both tests pass. They cover this certificate and exhaustive small-tournament
outcomes, including internal/boundary draws and losses, to check against false
impossibility labels. The native check confirms **380, 627, 32, 51, 87** and the
contradictory **4 versus 2** draw limits. One run measured about 1.9 ms for model
setup plus the supplied certificate; this is not a production full-matrix
latency measurement or a measured cost of finding useful subsets.
